# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective clients evaluating whether to hire the studio — founders, small-business owners, and operators who need a brand identity, a website, or ongoing content, and who are deciding if Aethel Labs is worth the spend. They arrive skeptical (they've seen agency sites that look good but say nothing) and are scanning for proof of craft and proof that one studio can do both design and build. The job to be done: decide, quickly and confidently, that this is the studio to start a project with — then start it.

## Product Purpose

A single-page studio site that demonstrates the studio's craft by *being* a piece of craft. It exists to convert a skeptical visitor into a project inquiry. Success = the visitor feels the quality in the first few seconds, scrolls the whole horizontal journey, and clicks "Start a project." The site is itself the strongest portfolio piece.

## Brand Personality

Spare, confident, noble-modern. Three words: **considered, crafted, worth**. Voice is plain and true — never hype ("revolutionary", "cutting-edge" are banned in copy), confident without shouting, short lines that let whitespace carry weight. A duality runs through everything: *noble* (considered, deliberate, built to last, "Old English æþel — noble, of worth") paired with *modern* (fast, ships, the stack the big names run on). Emotional goal: quiet awe, then trust. "Design worth the name."

## Anti-references

- Generic agency/template sites — the thing this explicitly is NOT ("a studio, not a template").
- SaaS-cream landing pages; warm-near-white "editorial" bodies.
- The hero-metric template (big number + stat grid + gradient accent).
- Decorative glassmorphism applied as default texture, gradient text, side-stripe borders.
- An uppercase tracked eyebrow / numbered marker above every section as scaffolding.
- Anything that feels stock, safe, or interchangeable with another studio's site.

## Design Principles

- **Practice what you preach.** The site must out-craft the work it shows; it is the proof, not a description of proof.
- **End to end, under one roof.** Design and build are inseparable here — the experience should feel singular and authored, never assembled from parts.
- **Every scroll matters.** Motion is structural, not decoration: scroll drives the brand mark's assembly, the mood, the reveals. Idle scrolling should still feel composed.
- **Spare, not sparse.** Restraint and whitespace carry weight — but the hero must feel *inhabited*, not empty. Few elements, each deliberate and alive.
- **Show, don't tell.** Let the artifact (the logo building itself, the cinematic journey) make the argument; keep copy short and true.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast for all body text (white-on-ink / ink-on-ivory; ember reserved for large/decorative use). `prefers-reduced-motion` is a first-class path: scroll-jacking, the logo build, and shard motion all collapse to a calm, fully-legible vertical layout. Keyboard-navigable (sidebar dots and form are reachable, visible focus). No content gated behind animations that may never fire on headless/inactive tabs.
